Behind the project

How this site came to be

  1. 2020–2021 · Lockdown

    From paper drawings to 3D

    During the COVID-19 lockdown, I set myself a challenge: model every WorldSkills CNC Milling test project in Siemens NX, starting from nothing but its orthographic drawing. It was much harder than I expected, so I asked experienced friends and the teachers who trained me at ITI for help.

    To read the drawings more easily, I had them photocopied at A3 size. Then I worked out every point with a calculator, sketched it on paper to check it, and measured it before building the part in 3D. I modelled every test project this way, and for one or two of them I also ran the machining simulation in NX. I still have those A3 copies; you can see each one on its project page. Most of my working was done on separate sheets, but a few pencil notes went straight onto the copies as I went.

    1. A3 photocopy
    2. Calculate + sketch
    3. 3D in NX
    4. Simulation (a few parts)
  2. 2020–2021 · Learning the web

    A first website, and a spinning part

    Next I wanted to share the parts online. I learned to build a static website with HTML, CSS and JavaScript from the web development playlist on the Brototype YouTube channel (called Crossroads back then). Then I wanted the parts in 3D, which led me to Three.js. It was hard to get at first. After searching through many tutorials, I managed one thing: a model that rotated on screen.

  3. 2026 · AI tools

    Finishing what I started

    In 2026 I started using AI tools like Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini. With Claude's help, the site became what I had in mind back then: all the controls that let you turn and study each part, and a cleaner, more attractive design throughout.

Six years on, I hope this helps you prepare for your own competition, or for your work on the shop floor.

— Machinist Hands
